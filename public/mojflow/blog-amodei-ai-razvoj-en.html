Amodei proposes slower AI development. What does it mean for your business?

The debate about the pace of AI development raises a practical question for businesses: how do more capable models become reliable everyday workflows?

MojFlow team · September 13, 2026

What is being proposed?

In We Must Pace the Frontier, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei proposes balancing frontier model progress with safety checks:

  1. Independent evaluators: ongoing access to teams and processes to assess safety. Anthropic commits to this step.
  2. Democratic coordination: shared standards and limits on unchecked progress, supported by governments.
  3. Global coordination: a broader agreement between governments, with compliance verification.

The steps need not happen in order. The proposal does not call for a complete halt to development.

How we read this news

For a business considering AI customer support, document processing or sales automation, it helps to separate two decisions. One concerns how the next generation of models is developed. The other concerns what a particular application can do with your data, money and customer relationships.

Our view: assess a business AI system through its actual workflow. A convincing chat response is insufficient evidence that an agent can independently modify orders or send quotations. That decision needs verifiable results and clearly assigned permissions.

The rest of this article is MojFlow’s analysis of business applications. Examples are illustrative; they are not part of Amodei’s plan or reported results from a particular client.

Example: an agent preparing a quotation

Imagine a customer requests 40 units of a product. An agent finds the catalog reference, checks the price list and prepares a draft. The team can verify the source of the quantity, price and delivery date before the quotation reaches the customer.

Now add a request: “Give us the same discount as last time.” If the previous agreement is unavailable, a responsible workflow sends the question to a salesperson. It does not turn an assumption into an approved discount. If the warehouse service does not respond, the draft should flag availability as unconfirmed.

Define that process before choosing a model. Our guide to preparing quotations with AI explores a more detailed example in Serbian.

Four questions before giving an agent more autonomy

  • What can it change? Separate reading data, drafting a result and executing an action. Specify who grants permission for each.
  • How do we verify the result? Prepare cases with known expected outcomes, including ambiguous requests, missing data and unavailable services.
  • Who handles a problem? Assign a person or team to uncertain cases, with enough context to continue the work.
  • How do we stop automation? Agree on a way to disable actions and return to manual processing while investigating an error.

These answers provide a more concrete basis for a decision than a promise that a system uses “the latest AI”.

A model change needs a workflow check

In our suggested rollout process, the same set of representative tasks is checked when the model, instructions or connected tool changes. This lets a team compare answer quality, corrections, processing time and cost per completed task.

A practical starting point is a limited pilot: one process, a defined dataset and someone reviewing results. Expand when the team has evidence that the workflow operates within agreed boundaries. Our guide to starting AI automation, in Serbian, covers planning that first step.

From a headline to a decision for your team

When assessing a system, ask to see both a successful case and a case where automation must stop. Who is notified? What information is preserved? Can the work continue without submitting everything again?
